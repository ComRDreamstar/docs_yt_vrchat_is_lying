# ToS Analysis: The Private Instance Question

**Scope:** What VRChat's Terms of Service actually say about adult content in private/locked instances, and whether the "tree falls in the forest" framing at **[37:49–38:25]** is a defensible reading.

**Source:** VRChat Terms of Service, full text. Quotations below are from the ToS itself, not from secondary summaries.

**Supersedes:** an earlier working note speculating that §8.4 was a licensing clause and might have been misapplied by the video. That was wrong — see §1.

---

## 1. The video's citations are correct ✅

At **[12:12–12:22]** the video cites **§13.2(d)** and **§8.4(c)**. Both are on point, and they are the right two clauses.

**§13.2(d) — Prohibited Conduct.** Users agree not to:

> Post, upload, or distribute any User Content or other content that is unlawful, defamatory, libelous, inaccurate, or that a reasonable person could deem to be objectionable, profane, indecent, pornographic, harassing, threatening, embarrassing, hateful, or otherwise inappropriate

**§8.4(c) — User Content Representations and Warranties.** By Posting, the user affirms that:

> your User Content could not be deemed by a reasonable person to be objectionable, profane, indecent, pornographic, harassing, threatening, embarrassing, hateful, or otherwise inappropriate. Uploading such User Content is also a violation of Section 13.2 of these Terms.

Note that **8.4(c) cross-references 13.2 itself** — the two clauses are explicitly linked in the document. The video's pairing is precise, not approximate.

*(For clarity: §8.4(a) is the ownership/licensing warranty. It is 8.4(c) that carries the content restriction. An earlier assumption that the video had cited a licensing provision by mistake was incorrect.)*

## 2. The private-instance defence fails on the text — three independent grounds

### 2.1 The prohibited act is *Posting*, not being observed

§8.1 defines the operative term broadly:

> Any User may leverage certain features of the Platform to develop content on or submit, upload, publish, broadcast, perform, or otherwise transmit content **to or via the Platform** […] ("Post")

The violation under 13.2(d) completes on transmission to the Platform. There is no witness element and no audience requirement. "If a tree falls in the forest and no one reports it" therefore does not engage the rule at all — it answers a question the clause does not ask.

Two further notes on the definition:
- It covers "**perform**" and "**broadcast**," not merely file upload. This arguably reaches conduct within an instance and not just the avatar asset. *(Interpretation, not something the text states outright — flag as such if used.)*
- It reads "to **or via** the Platform," which is broader than transmission to other users.

### 2.2 §13.4 explicitly contemplates private worlds

This is the most direct rebuttal in the document:

> VRChat reserves the right to monitor your use of the Platform (**whether or not you are in a private world, location or setting within the Platform**), including by making audio or video recordings of your activities on the Platform […] (b) if we reasonably believe such monitoring is necessary to investigate a violation of the Terms or applicable law

VRChat's own terms anticipate that violations occur in private spaces and expressly reserve the right to look. A private instance is not, on the document's own account, a place the rules stop applying.

### 2.3 No carve-out exists

The full text was searched for any instance-type or privacy exception to §13.2 or §8.4. **There is none.** Sections referencing "instance" concern licensing scope (8.2, 8.6), creator obligations (8.10), and support (§5). Nothing conditions the content prohibitions on instance type, lock state, or audience.

### 2.4 Corroborating: 18+ verification does not waive it

VRChat's own age-verification documentation states that 18+ verification and age-restricted instances **do not** waive the §13.2 policy on pornographic content. If a verified adults-only instance doesn't lift the rule, a private locked instance plainly doesn't either.

## 3. What the ToS does *not* settle

**Tupper's actual claim is about enforcement, not policy.** At **[46:53–47:03]**:

> The broadness is one thing. Enforcement is another thing. It's the separation between enforcement and policy.

Read carefully, he is not asserting that private ERP is permitted. He is describing a rule that exists and is not enforced. That is a coherent position, and it is a different claim from the one the video attributes to him.

So:

| Question | Settled by ToS? |
|---|---|
| Does the conduct violate the written rules, including in private instances? | ✅ **Yes** — unambiguously |
| Did the video cite the right clauses? | ✅ **Yes** — 13.2(d) and 8.4(c) |
| Is "tree falls in the forest" a defensible reading of the document? | ❌ **No** |
| Does deliberate non-enforcement amount to tacit permission? | ⬜ **Not a ToS question** — judgement call |
| Does private content demonstrably "seep" into public lobbies **[38:25–39:05]**? | ⬜ **Asserted, not shown** in the video |

The remaining disagreement is about **enforcement discretion**, and no reading of the contract resolves it.

## 4. Industry context — the fairest version of the counter-argument

This section exists because a reader who knows the history will raise it immediately, and the document is stronger for having addressed it first.

**Written prohibition plus selective enforcement is the industry norm, not a VRChat invention.** Adult content has appeared in every major shared virtual space — MUDs, Habbo Hotel, Second Life, WoW's unofficial ERP realms, IMVU, Roblox — including on platforms whose terms banned it outright. Very few have either permitted it openly or genuinely suppressed it.

There are non-cynical reasons for that posture:
- Enforcement at scale, across private spaces, is close to technically impossible without surveillance most users would reject
- A broad written rule preserves discretion to act on the cases that matter, which a narrow or absent rule would not
- Consensual adult conduct in adult-only contexts is not what moderation resources are best spent on

**Where the analogy breaks down.** The norm above is usually defended with the phrase *"between consenting adults."* On VRChat that premise is not established by the platform's architecture:

- The platform is rated **13+** and markets accordingly
- **Age assurance did not exist until November 2024**, arrived in early access January 2025, and remains **optional**
- Instances are therefore mixed by default, not adults-only by default

So the tolerated-in-practice standard from adults-only MMOs rests on a guarantee VRChat does not provide. That — not "adults do adult things," which is unremarkable and always has been — is the strongest form of the video's argument.

**And the strongest form of Tupper's.** That enforcement discretion is universal, that VRChat is being singled out for an industry-wide pattern, and that the alternative (aggressive enforcement inside private spaces) carries its own costs. The video's framing at **[15:27–15:42]** and **[1:01:29–1:02:03]** — that this is a uniquely rotten company — does not engage with this, and a reader who knows the history will notice.

No instance-type carve-out exists anywhere in the ToS.
