# Addendum — Chapter 8 Identification: Community Verification Result

*Append to `chapter8-identification-analysis.md`, or file alongside it.*

**Status:** Reported by community verification. Not independently verified by the author of this analysis — the underlying video was not reviewed here. Written as a reported finding, and should be presented that way until anyone relying on it has seen the material themselves.

## The dispositive item

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PtxWU7Ul-_k

**Video showing both accounts present and talking to each other.** Two parties cannot be one person in a recorded conversation between them.

This is precisely the test set out in §8 of the main analysis ("Concurrent presence — any instance, log, or recording placing both accounts online at the same time. This is dispositive and the community can check it"). The criterion was specified before the evidence surfaced, and it has now been met. That ordering matters: it wasn't a standard adjusted to fit a result.

**If the footage holds up, the identification at 56:35–1:00:24 is disproven** — not weakened, not unsupported, but false.

## The corroborating item

**A distinct difference in accent between the two speakers.** Fusl has a marked regional accent; the recorded FuzlTM does not.

Weight this carefully. It is corroborative, not dispositive, for two reasons:

- The video itself states a **voice changer** was in use at **[57:33–57:41]**. Voice changers alter pitch and timbre; accent and prosody typically survive them, but "typically" is doing real work in that sentence.
- Accent identification by ear is subjective and hard to argue about in a thread.

Lead with the concurrent video. Use the accent as support, never as the primary claim.

**A note on how to phrase this.** The accent point can be made without publishing where Fusl lives. "A distinct regional accent absent in the recorded speaker" carries the argument; naming a country of residence adds identifying detail about a private individual that the correction does not require. The purpose here is to *undo* an over-identification — reproducing the method while correcting it would be self-defeating.

## What this establishes, and what it does not

**Establishes:**
- FuzlTM and Fusl are different people
- The Chapter 8 identification — and the on-screen "They found you, Fusl" at **[56:35]** — is wrong
- A third party was publicly presented, before a large audience, as someone they are not, with their job, avatar preferences, and gender identity offered as corroborating evidence

**Does not establish anything about:**
- Any underlying allegation against Fusl from the October 2025 documentary
- The rebuttal analysis at **[50:28–56:27]** (internal investigation, outside counsel, Matrix device count, Steam achievements, Ruikawa, deleted tweets), which never depended on the identification and stands or falls on its own — see §1 of the main analysis
- Whether Fusl's GitHub response was adequate

Keep these separate. A failed identification does not resolve the other questions in either direction, and conflating them repeats the structural error the chapter made.

## The video's own footage already pointed here

Worth restating, because it means this was findable without any new evidence:

- **[57:49–58:21]** The speaker discusses Fusl in the third person for roughly thirty seconds, sympathetically, including *"I really can't tell you where she hangs out for her safety"* — protecting a third party's location
- **[58:21–58:29]** The interviewer asks how *"you and other Fusl"* met, a question presupposing two people; the answer is *"I can't comment on this"*
- **[58:59]** The speaker says *"I'm not Fus—"* outright
- **[58:59–59:12]** The speaker volunteers the resemblance (shared name, avatar type) as coincidence — the video re-presents this single sentence as multiple independent matches

All three appear in the footage the chapter uses to prove the identification, and none is addressed on screen.

## Consequence for the chapter

Sections **56:35–1:00:24** collapse. Every inference in that stretch was drawn from a premise now contradicted:

- The lobby confrontation at **[59:28–59:42]** — leaving is the expected behaviour of someone wrongly accused, not evidence of guilt
- The privacy complaint at **[59:42–1:00:05]** — a misidentified third party would have standing to file one, and a first-time complainant is a plausible reason it succeeded where earlier attempts failed
- "That is a hell of a coincidence" at **[59:12–59:22]** — was a coincidence

## A note on presenting this

Two people are affected, and one of them appears to have been entirely uninvolved.

State the finding as *"the identification is disproven."* Not as a counter-accusation, and not as an occasion to investigate who FuzlTM actually is. The correction needs no further identifying detail about either party than has already been published — and given that the original error broadcast a private individual's gender identity as evidence, restraint here is the whole point rather than an afterthought.

If the main document is published, this correction should be **as visible as the original claim it corrects**, not a footnote.
